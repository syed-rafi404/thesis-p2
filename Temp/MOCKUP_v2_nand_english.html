NAND Gate: AND Followed by NOT

Written by Claude, by hand, as a target for the pipeline. The board picture, the six numbered boxes and their names come from the real run; the quotes are word for word from transcript_loso.txt. Every sentence around them is Claude's, not model output.

The second universal gate of this lecture: what NAND means, how it is built from an AND gate and a NOT gate, how its truth table is filled in column by column, and what its circuit symbol looks like.

Key takeaways

  • NAND means AND followed by NOT: take the AND of the two inputs, then invert the result.
  • The output is 0 only when both inputs are 1. Every other row gives 1.
  • NAND is a universal gate: any other logic gate can be built from NAND gates alone.
  • The lecturer fills the table in two passes: first the AND column, then its inverse.

Building the NAND gate, step by step

In one line: A NAND gate is an AND gate with a NOT gate on its output.

Boxes: 1 Title - 2 NAND gate - 3 Definition - 4 Block diagram - 5 Truth table - 6 Gate symbol

The gate is named in the blue box 2, and the orange box 3 gives the whole definition in four characters: = AND + NOT. Read it as an instruction in two steps. Take the inputs A and B, AND them together, then invert whatever comes out.

The green box 4 draws those two steps as a block. A and B enter on the left, the AND happens inside the block, and what leaves on the right is A.B with a bar over it. The bar is the NOT.

The truth table (purple box 5)

The lecturer builds this table one column at a time, not one row at a time.

ABA.B (AND)(A.B)' (NAND)
0001
0101
1001
1110

First the third column: A AND B, which is just multiplication, so it is 0 in every row except the last. Then the fourth column: invert the third. Three zeros become ones, and the single one becomes zero.

That last column is the NAND output, and it is worth saying out loud what it shows: NAND gives 0 only when both inputs are 1. It is AND turned upside down.

The lecturer said: "First I have to AND them. What was AND? Multiplication. So A times B. And
after that, what do I do? I take the NOT."
The lecturer said: "This column here - this one is my NAND gate's output."

The circuit symbol (pink box 6)

The pink box 6 is how you draw it: the AND gate's shape with a small circle on its output. That little circle is the NOT. When you see it in a circuit diagram, you are looking at a NAND gate.

Background (not said in the lecture)

A gate is called universal when every other logic gate can be built out of copies of it alone, and NAND is one of only two gates with that property; NOR, from the previous board, is the other. This is why chip manufacturers care about it: a factory that can etch one reliable NAND cell can build any circuit from that one part. Inverting a gate's output is also why the small circle appears again and again in circuit diagrams - it always means "and then NOT".

Remember: NAND outputs 0 only when both inputs are 1; in every other case it outputs 1.

Check yourself

  1. In your own words, what two steps does a NAND gate perform?
  2. For inputs A = 1 and B = 0, what does the NAND gate output, and why?
  3. Which single row of the truth table separates NAND from AND?
  4. What does the small circle on the gate symbol mean?
Show answers
  1. It ANDs the two inputs, then inverts the result: AND first, NOT second.
  2. It outputs 1. The AND of 1 and 0 is 0, and inverting 0 gives 1.
  3. The last row, A = 1 and B = 1: AND gives 1 there, NAND gives 0.
  4. The circle is the NOT. Without it the symbol would be a plain AND gate.
