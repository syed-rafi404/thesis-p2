InsightLens: A Vision-Language Based Framework for Extracting and Understanding Classroom Content in Dual Languages

Abstract

Many university lectures in Bangladesh are delivered in Banglish, a code-mixed blend of Bengali and English, and much of their content is written on the whiteboard. Standard speech recognition handles code-mixed speech poorly, and the worked examples, code and numbers on the board are lost to anyone reviewing a recording. We present InsightLens, a framework that turns a classroom video into structured lecture notes by combining speech recognition, whiteboard reading with a vision-language model, and summarisation with a large language model. Each stage is evaluated separately on 13 recorded lectures by three lecturers. For speech, Whisper large-v3-turbo is fine-tuned with low-rank adaptation (LoRA) and tested leave-one-speaker-out, so that every lecturer is evaluated without having been heard in training. Trained on 80 to 114 minutes of speech from two lecturers, the model lowers the character error rate on the third from 72.8% to 50.3% and the word error rate from 95.3% to 75.7%; all six runs are statistically significant (p < 0.00001), although absolute error remains high. For the whiteboard, prompting Qwen2.5-VL for a full transcription instead of keywords raises the recall of hand-verified board content from 31.2% to 88.8% across 35 boards, recovering 65 of 67 written numbers that the keyword approach missed entirely, and the model reads a third lecturer's boards at 89.7%. Notes generated with this board content contain 88.0% of it, against 37.2% for the original pipeline. We also report negative results: fusing visual cues into speech recognition gives no significant improvement, and a term-based F1 metric proves unreliable for code-mixed speech, varying by 8 points between identical training runs. The results indicate that vision-language board reading and small amounts of targeted speech data, rather than cross-modal fusion, are what make dual-language classroom content recoverable.

Keywords: code-mixed speech recognition, Banglish, Whisper, low-rank adaptation, vision-language models, whiteboard transcription, lecture note generation
