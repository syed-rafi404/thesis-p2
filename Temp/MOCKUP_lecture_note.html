Lecture 7: Universal gates

Digital Logic Design · the NAND gate, 4:40–7:00

The NAND gate

In one line: a NAND gate is an AND gate followed by a NOT gate. The lecturer writes exactly that on the board: NAND = AND + NOT orange box 3.

How it works

Look at the block diagram in the blue box 4. Two inputs, A and B, go into the NAND box, and the result comes out on the right:

  1. First, AND. The gate multiplies the inputs: A · B.
  2. Then, NOT. It flips the result. That is why the lecturer draws one bar over the whole AB: the output is (AB)′, “not (A and B)”.
“and mane ami kichilo multiplication. so a into b. erpor ami ki korbo? not korbo. so ab er upore ekta whole bar”
The lecturer: AND means multiplication, so A times B. Then what do I do? NOT. So one bar over the whole AB.

The truth table

The lecturer gets the NAND column by flipping every AB value:

ABABNAND
0001
0101
1001
1110
“lastly ei one ta hoechebe ki? zero. so ei je column ta, ei ta hocche amar nand gate er output.”
The lecturer: and this last 1 becomes what? 0. So this column is the NAND gate's output.

Remember: a NAND gate gives 0 only when both inputs are 1. In every other case it gives 1.

The symbol

The green box 6 is the gate symbol; the small circle at the output is the NOT. Heads up: the shape drawn on this board has the curved back of an OR gate. The standard NAND symbol has the flat back of an AND gate, with the same circle.

Where this fits

This lecture covers the two universal gates, NOR and NAND; NAND is the second one.

Background, not said in this lecture: “universal” means any other gate can be built from NAND gates alone.

Check yourself

  1. What does a NAND gate output when A = 1 and B = 1?
  2. Why is the bar drawn over the whole AB, not over A and B separately?
Show answers
  1. 0. It is the only input pair that gives 0.
  2. Because the NOT is applied after the AND, to the result AB. Separate bars (A′B′) would be a different gate.
