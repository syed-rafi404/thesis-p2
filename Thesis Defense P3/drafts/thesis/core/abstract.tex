\section*{Abstract}

University teaching in Bangladesh is usually delivered in a mixture of Bengali and
English, and students write that mixture in the Roman alphabet, a form commonly called
Banglish. Existing speech recognition does not serve it. A multilingual model returns an
English translation of what was said, and a Bengali model returns Bengali script that no
student in the class writes. The whiteboard, which carries most of the worked examples in
a class taught without slides, is lost in both cases.

This research presents InsightLens, a four stage system that converts a recorded Banglish
whiteboard lecture into a lecture note. Whisper large-v3-turbo adapted with Low-Rank
Adaptation produces a romanised Banglish transcript. Each whiteboard is rebuilt from the
video by taking every tile of the image from a moment when the lecturer was not standing
in front of it, so no pixel is synthesised. A vision-language model then names and
transcribes numbered regions of the rebuilt board, and a language model writes one
section of notes per board with every quotation checked against the transcript.

A corpus of 44 lectures from three lecturers was recorded and 5.15 hours of it was
transcribed by hand. On six held out lectures, fine-tuning reduces the median per-clip
character error rate from 67.7 to 15.8 per cent across two random seeds. Changing the
board reading prompt raises recall of hand-verified board content from 31.2 to 88.8 per
cent, while a larger model is worth 5.3 points. The generated notes carry 89.1 per cent
of the board content against 37.2 per cent for a conventional summarisation prompt, and
in a blind pilot twenty readers preferred them to the earlier notes, 15 of the 19 who
expressed a preference. Four
approaches that failed are reported with the same detail, and the evaluation shows that
measures built from English word lists systematically favour a model that translates
code-mixed speech over one that transcribes it.

\textbf{Keywords:} Code-mixed speech recognition; Banglish; Bengali-English; Low-Rank
